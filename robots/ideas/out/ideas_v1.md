### Idea 1: **Current Flow Simulator** (विद्युत धारा सिम्युलेटर)
1. **Student Experience:**  
   - Screen 1: A wire with electrons moving slowly.  
   - Screen 2: Add a bulb, electrons slow further.  
   - Screen 3: Introduce a battery, electrons speed up.  
   - Screen 4: Increase resistance, electrons slow again.  
   - Screen 5: Tap to see conventional current direction (opposite to electron flow).  
   - Screen 6: MCQ: "Which direction does conventional current flow?"  
2. **Concept:** Current and electron flow.  
3. **Visual:**  
   - Wire with blue dots (electrons) moving. Battery (red and black terminals), bulb (glows when electrons pass). Labels: "Electron flow," "Conventional current."  
4. **Interaction:** Drag the battery voltage slider. Electrons speed up or slow down. Bulb brightness changes.  
5. **Trap:** Students confuse electron flow (negative to positive) with conventional current (positive to negative).  
6. **Why Remember:** Seeing electrons move and bulb react makes current tangible.

### Idea 2: **Resistance Maze** (प्रतिरोध चक्रव्यूह)  
1. **Student Experience:**  
   - Screen 1: A maze with thick and thin paths.  
   - Screen 2: Electrons move faster in thick paths (low resistance).  
   - Screen 3: Add a resistor, electrons slow.  
   - Screen 4: MCQ: "Which path has higher resistance?"  
   - Screen 5: Tap to vary resistor value, see electron speed change.  
   - Screen 6: Real-world analogy: "Think of thick pipes vs thin pipes."  
2. **Concept:** Resistance.  
3. **Visual:**  
   Maze with thick (blue) and thin (red) paths. Electron dots move faster/slower. Labels: "Thick path = Low resistance," "Thin path = High resistance."  
4. **Interaction:** Tap to add resistors; watch electron speed decrease.  
5. **Trap:** Students think resistance depends only on material, not thickness or length.  
6. **Why Remember:** Maze analogy makes resistance intuitive.  

---

### Idea 3: **Voltage Climb** (वोल्टेज चढ़ाई)  
1. **Student Experience:**  
   - Screen 1: A hill labeled "Potential Difference."  
   - Screen 2: Electrons climb the hill with energy from the battery.  
   - Screen 3: Add a bulb, electrons "fall" down, releasing energy as light.  
   - Screen 4: MCQ: "What happens to energy as electrons fall?"  
   - Screen 5: Drag slider to vary hill height (voltage).  
   - Screen 6: Real-world analogy: "Think of water flowing downhill."  
2. **Concept:** Potential difference and energy transfer.  
3. **Visual:**  
   Hill with electrons climbing. Bulb at the bottom glows when electrons fall. Labels: "Hill height = Voltage," "Energy released as light."  
4. **Interaction:** Drag slider to change hill height; bulb brightness changes.  
5. **Trap:** Students confuse voltage (energy per charge) with current (rate of flow).  
6. **Why Remember:** Hill analogy links voltage to energy.  

---

### Idea 4: **Series vs Parallel Race** (श्रेणी बनाम समानांतर दौड़)  
1. **Student Experience:**  
   - Screen 1: Two circuits: series (one path) and parallel (two paths).  
   - Screen 2: Electrons race in both circuits simultaneously.  
   - Screen 3: Bulbs glow brighter in parallel.  
   - Screen 4: MCQ: "Which circuit has brighter bulbs?"  
   - Screen 5: Tap to add resistors, see current split in parallel.  
   - Screen 6: Real-world analogy: "Think of traffic lanes."  
2. **Concept:** Series and parallel circuits.  
3. **Visual:**  
   Side-by-side circuits: series (one bulb) and parallel (two bulbs). Electrons moving in sync. Labels: "Series = Single path," "Parallel = Multiple paths."  
4. **Interaction:** Tap to add resistors; see current split in parallel, bulb brightness change.  
5. **Trap:** Students think adding resistors always reduces current, even in parallel.  
6. **Why Remember:** Race analogy makes series vs parallel clear.  

---

### Idea 5: **Power Meter** (पावर मीटर)  
1. **Student Experience:**  
   - Screen 1: A heater with dials for voltage and current.  
   - Screen 2: Drag sliders for voltage (230 V) and current (5 A).  
   - Screen 3: Power (P = VI) updates in real-time.  
   - Screen 4: MCQ: "What happens if voltage doubles?"  
   - Screen 5: Add cost per unit (₹6), calculate energy bill.  
   - Screen 6: Real-world analogy: "Think of car engine power."  
2. **Concept:** Power and energy.  
3. **Visual:**  
   Heater with dials labeled "Voltage" and "Current." Power meter updates as sliders move. Labels: "Power = Voltage × Current."  
4. **Interaction:** Drag sliders for voltage and current; watch power update.  
5. **Trap:** Students forget to convert mA to A in calculations.  
6. **Why Remember:** Real-time power calculation links theory to home appliances.  

---

### Idea 6: **Fuse Blow-Up** (फ्यूज उड़ाना)  
1. **Student Experience:**  
   - Screen 1: A home circuit with live, neutral, and fuse.  
   - Screen 2: Drag slider for current (1 A to 10 A).  
   - Screen 3: Fuse blows at 5 A, circuit breaks.  
   - Screen 4: MCQ: "Why does a fuse blow?"  
   - Screen 5: Add MCB, reset circuit.  
   - Screen 6: Real-world analogy: "Think of a circuit breaker."  
2. **Concept:** Fuse and safety.  
3. **Visual:**  
   Home circuit with fuse (thin wire). Labels: "Fuse rating = 5 A," "MCB protection."  
4. **Interaction:** Drag slider for current; fuse blows at 5 A, MCB trips.  
5. **Trap:** Students think fuses protect devices, not circuits.  
6. **Why Remember:** Seeing fuse blow teaches circuit protection.  

---

### Recommended Flow:  
1. **Current Flow Simulator** (understand electrons and current).  
2. **Resistance Maze** (learn resistance basics).  
3. **Voltage Climb** (relate voltage to energy).  
4. **Series vs Parallel Race** (compare circuits).  
5. **Power Meter** (calculate power and cost).  
6. **Fuse Blow-Up** (safety and practical applications).
