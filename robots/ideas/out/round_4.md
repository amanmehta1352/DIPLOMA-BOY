### **B. Numerical Types with Method B**

1. **Charge from Current and Time:**  
   **Method B:** Think of current as "charge per second": 2A means 2C per second. In 5s, charge = 2 × 5 = 10C.  

2. **Number of Electrons from Charge:**  
   **Method B:** Each electron has 1.6 × 10^-19 C. Number = Total charge / Charge per electron = 10 / (1.6 × 10^-19) = 6.25 × 10^19.  

3. **Work from V and Q:**  
   **Method B:** Voltage = Work per unit charge ⇒ Work = Voltage × Charge = 6 × 10 = 60J.  

4. **Resistance from Resistivity:**  
   **Method B:** Resistance increases with length and decreases with area ⇒ R = (small resistivity × long length) / large area = (1.7 × 10^-8 × 2) / (1 × 10^-6) = 0.034Ω.  

5. **Resistivity from Data:**  
   **Method B:** Resistivity = Resistance × Area / Length = (0.03 × 2 × 10^-6) / 3 = 2 × 10^-8 Ωm.  

6. **Stretched/Folded Wire:**  
   **Method B:** Folding doubles the area and halves the length ⇒ R_new = R_original / 4 = 4Ω / 4 = 1Ω.  

7. **Series Resistance:**  
   **Method B:** Series resistance = sum of individual resistances ⇒ 2 + 3 + 5 = 10Ω.  

8. **Parallel Resistance:**  
   **Method B:** For two resistors, R_parallel = product / sum = (2 × 4)/(2 + 4) = 8/6 = 1.33Ω.  

9. **Mixed Series-Parallel:**  
   **Method B:** First find parallel equivalent (8/3Ω), then add series resistor (2Ω) ⇒ 2 + 8/3 = 14/3Ω.  

10. **Maximum and Minimum Resistance from n Resistors:**  
   **Method B:** Maximum = all in series (1 + 2 + 3 = 6Ω); Minimum = all in parallel = 1/(1/1 + 1/2 + 1/3) = 6/11Ω.  

11. **Power of Appliance and Current/Fuse Choice:**  
   **Method B:** Current = Power / Voltage = 1500/230 ≈ 6.52A ⇒ Use 10A fuse.  

12. **Bulbs in Series vs Parallel Brightness:**  
   **Method B:** Higher resistance bulb glows brighter in series ⇒ 60W bulb (higher R) glows brighter.  

13. **Heat by Joule's Law:**  
   **Method B:** Heat ∝ current² ⇒ H = (2)^2 × 3 × 5 = 60J.  

14. **Energy Cost in kWh:**  
   **Method B:** Energy = Power × Time = 1.5 × 2 × 30 = 90 kWh; Cost = 90 × 6 = ₹540.  

15. **Resistance of a Bulb from Its Rating:**  
   **Method B:** Resistance = Voltage² / Power = 230² / 60 ≈ 881.67Ω.  

16. **Folded Wire:**  
   **Example:** A 4Ω wire is folded into half. Find its new resistance.  
   **Method A:** Folding halves length and doubles area ⇒ R_new = R_original / 4 = 4/4 = 1Ω.  
   **Method B:** Resistance ∝ length / area ⇒ Halving length and doubling area reduces R by 4 times.  
   **Answer:** 1Ω.  

17. **Compare Two Resistors from V-I Graph Slopes:**  
   **Example:** Two resistors have V-I graphs with slopes 2Ω and 4Ω. Compare their resistance.  
   **Method A:** Slope = Resistance ⇒ R1 = 2Ω, R2 = 4Ω ⇒ R2 > R1.  
   **Method B:** Steeper slope = higher resistance ⇒ R2 > R1.  
   **Answer:** R2 > R1.

### **C. Exam Lines**

1. **1 Coulomb:** "One coulomb is the charge transferred by a current of one ampere in one second."  
2. **1 Ampere:** "One ampere is the current when one coulomb of charge flows per second."  
3. **1 Volt:** "One volt is the potential difference when one joule of work is done to move one coulomb of charge."  
4. **1 Ohm:** "One ohm is the resistance when one volt produces a current of one ampere."  
5. **1 Watt:** "One watt is the power when one joule of energy is transferred per second."  
6. **1 kWh:** "One kilowatt-hour is the energy consumed when one kilowatt of power is used for one hour (3.6 × 10^6 J)."  
7. **Ohm's Law:** "Ohm's law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it, provided temperature remains constant."  
8. **Resistivity:** "Resistivity is the resistance of a material of unit length and unit cross-sectional area."  
9. **Joule's Law:** "Joule's law states that the heat produced in a resistor is directly proportional to the square of the current, resistance, and time."  
10. **Parallel Connection:** "Appliances in homes are connected in parallel so each gets full voltage and operates independently."

### **D. Four Short "Why" Explanations**

1. **Alloys in Heating Elements:** "Alloys like nichrome have high resistivity and melting points, making them ideal for heating elements as they can withstand high temperatures without melting."  
2. **Tungsten in Filaments:** "Tungsten is used in filaments because of its high melting point and ability to glow brightly at high temperatures without burning. The bulb is filled with inert gas to prevent oxidation."  
3. **Fuse Working:** "A fuse is connected in series with the circuit and has a low melting point. If current exceeds the fuse rating, it melts, breaking the circuit and protecting appliances."  
4. **Connecting Cord:** "The connecting cord has low resistance and carries the same current as the appliance. Since heat = I²R and R is low, the cord does not get hot."

### **E. Activity/Experiment Questions**

1. **Verifying Ohm's Law:**  
   **Activity:** Set up a circuit with a battery, rheostat, ammeter, voltmeter, and resistor. Record V and I for different resistances.  
   **Observation:** V/I ratio remains constant.  
   **Conclusion:** Ohm's law is verified as V ∝ I.  

2. **Resistance Depends on Length:**  
   **Activity:** Use wires of different lengths but the same material and thickness. Measure their resistance.  
   **Observation:** Resistance increases with length.  
   **Conclusion:** Resistance is directly proportional to length.
