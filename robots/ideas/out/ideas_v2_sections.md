### Section 1: Electric Charge and Current  
#### Title: Electric Charge and Current / विद्युत आवेश और धारा  
#### Hook: Why does your phone charger heat up when plugged in?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Electric charge is the property of matter that causes it to experience a force in an electric field.  
- Current is the flow of charge, measured in amperes (A).  
- Conventional current flows from positive to negative, while electrons move in the opposite direction.  

#### Figure Spec (F1):  
- Left: A battery (square) with (+) and (-) terminals.  
- Right: A conductor (thin rectangle) with electrons (small circles) moving left to right.  
- Top: Conventional current arrow (red) pointing right.  
- Labels:  
  - Electrons: "e⁻" (black).  
  - Conventional Current: "I" (red).  
- Interactive: Tap the battery to show electron movement and conventional current arrows.  

#### Interaction:  
- Tap the battery to toggle electron movement and conventional current arrows. Formula: \( I = \frac{Q}{t} \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** A charge of 2 C flows through a wire in 4 s. Find the current.  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( I = \frac{Q}{t} = \frac{2\,C}{4\,s} = 0.5\,A \).  
**Method B (Unit):**  
Charge per second: \( \frac{2\,C}{4\,s} = 0.5\,C/s = 0.5\,A \).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Using time as charge. Correct: Current is charge divided by time.  

#### MCQ:  
What is the current if 5 C flows in 10 s?  
(A) 0.2 A  
(B) 0.5 A  
(C) 2 A  
(D) 50 A  
**Answer:** (B)

### Section 2: Potential Difference and Circuit Symbols  
#### Title: Potential Difference and Circuit Symbols / विभवांतर और परिपथ चिन्ह  
#### Hook: Why does a voltmeter read 230 V across a heater but 0 V across a switch?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Potential difference (V) is the work done to move a unit charge between two points.  
- Voltmeter is connected in parallel; ammeter is connected in series.  
- Symbols: battery, resistor, switch, bulb, voltmeter, ammeter.  

#### Figure Spec (F2):  
- A simple circuit with a battery (square), resistor (zigzag), bulb (circle), and switch (line gap).  
- Voltmeter (V) connected in parallel across the bulb.  
- Ammeter (A) connected in series with the bulb.  
- Labels:  
  - Voltmeter: "V" (blue).  
  - Ammeter: "A" (red).  
- Interactive: Tap the voltmeter to show its reading (230 V).  

#### Interaction:  
- Tap the voltmeter to show its reading. Formula: \( V = \frac{W}{Q} \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** A 100 J work is done to move 5 C charge. Find the potential difference.  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( V = \frac{W}{Q} = \frac{100\,J}{5\,C} = 20\,V \).  
**Method B (Unit):**  
Energy per charge: \( \frac{100\,J}{5\,C} = 20\,J/C = 20\,V \).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Connecting the voltmeter in series. Correct: Voltmeter is always in parallel.  

#### MCQ:  
What is the potential difference if 200 J is done to move 10 C?  
(A) 10 V  
(B) 20 V  
(C) 200 V  
(D) 2000 V  
**Answer:** (B)

### Section 3: Ohm’s Law and V-I Graph  
#### Title: Ohm’s Law and V-I Graph / ओम का नियम और V-I ग्राफ  
#### Hook: Why does your bulb glow brighter when voltage increases?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Ohm’s Law: \( V = IR \). Current is directly proportional to voltage.  
- V-I graph for ohmic devices is a straight line through the origin.  

#### Figure Spec (F3):  
- Graph: V (y-axis, blue), I (x-axis, red).  
- A straight line through the origin labelled "Ohmic Resistor."  
- Labels:  
  - X-axis: "I (A)" (red).  
  - Y-axis: "V (V)" (blue).  
- Interactive: Drag the line to change slope (resistance).  

#### Interaction:  
- Drag the line to change slope (resistance). Formula: \( R = \frac{V}{I} \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** A resistor has a voltage of 10 V and current of 2 A. Find its resistance.  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{10\,V}{2\,A} = 5\,\Omega \).  
**Method B (Ratio):**  
Voltage is 10 V for 2 A, so resistance is \( \frac{10}{2} = 5\,\Omega \).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Using V and I inversely. Correct: \( R = \frac{V}{I} \).  

#### MCQ:  
What is the resistance if V = 15 V and I = 3 A?  
(A) 3 Ω  
(B) 5 Ω  
(C) 15 Ω  
(D) 45 Ω  
**Answer:** (B)

### Section 4: Series and Parallel Resistors  
#### Title: Series and Parallel Resistors / श्रेणी और समानांतर प्रतिरोध  
#### Hook: Why does adding bulbs in series make them dimmer but in parallel keeps them bright?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Series: Same current, total resistance \( R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 \).  
- Parallel: Same voltage, total resistance \( \frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \).  

#### Figure Spec (F4):  
- Left: Two resistors in series with one path.  
- Right: Two resistors in parallel with two paths.  
- Labels:  
  - Series: "R₁ + R₂" (black).  
  - Parallel: "1/R₁ + 1/R₂" (black).  
- Interactive: Tap resistors to toggle between series and parallel.  

#### Interaction:  
- Tap resistors to toggle between series and parallel. Formula: Series \( R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 \); Parallel \( \frac{1}{R_{total}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** Two resistors, 2 Ω and 3 Ω, are connected in series. Find the total resistance.  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( R_{total} = R_1 + R_2 = 2\,\Omega + 3\,\Omega = 5\,\Omega \).  
**Method B (Unit):**  
Resistances add directly: \( 2 + 3 = 5\,\Omega \).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Adding resistors in parallel directly. Correct: Use reciprocal formula.  

#### MCQ:  
What is the total resistance if 4 Ω and 4 Ω are in parallel?  
(A) 1 Ω  
(B) 2 Ω  
(C) 4 Ω  
(D) 8 Ω  
**Answer:** (B)

### Section 5: Heating Effect and Joule’s Law  
#### Title: Heating Effect and Joule’s Law / तापीय प्रभाव और जूल का नियम  
#### Hook: Why does a heater coil glow red but the connecting wires stay cool?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Heat produced: \( H = I^2Rt \).  
- Applications: heater, fuse, bulb filament.  

#### Figure Spec (F5):  
- A heater coil (zigzag) glowing red.  
- Labels:  
  - Coil: "Heater Coil" (red).  
  - Heat: "H = I²Rt" (black).  
- Interactive: Tap the coil to show heat production.  

#### Interaction:  
- Tap the coil to show heat production. Formula: \( H = I^2Rt \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** A heater has a current of 5 A, resistance of 10 Ω, and runs for 2 s. Find the heat produced.  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( H = I^2Rt = (5\,A)^2 \times 10\,\Omega \times 2\,s = 500\,J \).  
**Method B (Unit):**  
\( 5^2 \times 10 \times 2 = 500\,J \).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Using \( H = VIt \) instead of \( H = I^2Rt \). Correct: Use \( H = I^2Rt \).  

#### MCQ:  
What is the heat if I = 3 A, R = 4 Ω, and t = 5 s?  
(A) 60 J  
(B) 120 J  
(C) 180 J  
(D) 360 J  
**Answer:** (C)

### Section 6: Electric Power and kWh  
#### Title: Electric Power and kWh / विद्युत शक्ति और kWh  
#### Hook: Why does a 100 W bulb consume more units than a 40 W bulb?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Power: \( P = VI \). Unit: watt (W).  
- Energy: \( E = Pt \). Commercial unit: kWh.  

#### Figure Spec (F6):  
- A bulb labelled "100 W" connected to a power meter showing "kWh."  
- Labels:  
  - Bulb: "P = VI" (black).  
  - Meter: "kWh" (blue).  
- Interactive: Tap the bulb to show power consumption.  

#### Interaction:  
- Tap the bulb to show power consumption. Formula: \( E = Pt \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** A 100 W bulb runs for 5 hours. Find the energy consumed in kWh.  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( E = Pt = 100\,W \times 5\,h = 0.5\,kWh \).  
**Method B (Unit):**  
\( \frac{100}{1000} \times 5 = 0.5\,kWh \).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Using hours instead of seconds. Correct: Convert W to kW and use hours.  

#### MCQ:  
What is the energy if P = 50 W and t = 10 h?  
(A) 0.5 kWh  
(B) 5 kWh  
(C) 50 kWh  
(D) 500 kWh  
**Answer:** (A)

### Section 7: Domestic Wiring and Safety  
#### Title: Domestic Wiring and Safety / घरेलू वायरिंग और सुरक्षा  
#### Hook: Why does a fuse blow when too many appliances are used?  
#### Core Idea:  
- Live (brown), neutral (blue), earth (green/yellow).  
- Fuse and MCB protect against overload.  

#### Figure Spec (F7):  
- A house wiring diagram: live (brown), neutral (blue), earth (green/yellow), fuse (rectangle), MCB (square), and switch (line gap).  
- Labels:  
  - Fuse: "Fuse" (red).  
  - MCB: "MCB" (blue).  
- Interactive: Tap the fuse to show it blowing.  

#### Interaction:  
- Tap the fuse to show it blowing. Formula: \( P = VI \).  

#### Example:  
**Q:** A fuse is rated 5 A. Can it handle a 1000 W heater at 230 V?  
**Method A (Formula):**  
\( I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{1000\,W}{230\,V} = 4.35\,A \) (Yes).  
**Method B (Unit):**  
\( \frac{1000}{230} = 4.35\,A \) (Yes).  

#### Common Mistake:  
Mistake: Ignoring fuse rating. Correct: Ensure current < fuse rating.  

#### MCQ:  
What is the current for a 2000 W heater at 230 V?  
(A) 8.7 A  
(B) 10 A  
(C) 20 A  
(D) 23 A  
**Answer:** (A)
